# The goal
You are a whole civilization. You choose where to build, what to research, where to settle, whom to trade with and whom to fight. Your people do the daily work by themselves: they take the free places in your buildings, gather around their settlements and feed everyone once a minute. Nobody else gives them orders.

Time runs all the time. You can pause (Space) whenever you like and give orders while paused.

## How a game is won
The goal is set in the start menu:
- **First to the Middle Ages:** be the first civilization to enter the Middle Ages.
- **Last civilization standing:** every other civilization is gone.
- **Best score after 2 hours:** the score counts people, land, technologies and the army.

A civilization loses when it has nobody left: no settlement and no unit. A game to the Middle Ages takes about an hour.

## The start
With "Choose where we start" on, the civilizations pick their start in turns. First the map shows only the land for a moment, then you have 30 seconds to click a hex for your first camp. You cannot start on water or mountains, nor inside the circles around the starts already taken.

# The screen
- **Top left:** our goods. The number is the stock, the green or red number how much comes or goes in a minute. Hover a good to see who makes and who uses it.
- **Top middle:** the era, how many technologies we still need for the next one and what we are researching. Click it to open the research tree.
- **Top right:** the time of the game, the speed, Routes, the help (?), this manual, the tutorial, Save and Menu.
- **Right:** the advisor (when we have one) and the alerts. An alert says what needs us and what to do; click it (or press `J`) to go there.
- **Left:** the side panel of what is selected: a settlement, a building, a unit or a hex. Its buttons are the orders for it.
- **Bottom:** the building bar. Its tabs are the kinds of buildings; its slots the buildings we can build now. Next to the tabs are the six windows.
- **Bottom right:** the chronicle, what happened lately. "more" shows a longer piece.

## The windows
| 1 | Settlements: all our settlements, their people, food, homes and problems.
| 2 | Research: the tree of technologies; pick the next research here.
| 3 | Civilizations: the civilizations we know, war, peace, treaties and exchange of goods.
| 4 | Thinkers: our thinker and advisor, and the ones we may hire.
| 5 | Summary: how the game goes, civilization by civilization, with a timeline.
| 6 | Units: all our units and patrols; stop, resume or call them back.
| T | Tutorial: the first steps as a task list.

# Controls
Everything in the game can be done with the mouse. The keys below do the same faster, and the keyboard cursor (Enter) reaches every button of the screen without the mouse.

## Map and camera
| W A S D or arrows | Move the map.
| Mouse wheel | Zoom where the mouse points.
| Middle mouse button | Drag the map.
| Home | Back to what is selected (or to our first settlement).

## Time
| Space | Pause and go on.
| + / - | Faster and slower (pause, 1x, 2x, 4x).
| F5 | Save the game. It also saves itself every 5 minutes; load from the start menu.

## Selecting and giving orders
| Left click | Select a settlement, building, unit or hex. Its panel opens on the left.
| Left click again | On the same hex: picks what stands on it (units).
| Shift + left click | Add the units on a hex to the selection.
| Right click | Send the selected units there. On an enemy: attack.
| Right button held | Draw the way over the hexes: they walk exactly that way.
| Q / E | The previous or next of our settlements.
| R / Shift + R | The next or previous of our units.
| J | The next alert: goes to the place it is about.
| Esc | Cancel what is going on, close the window, unselect. With nothing open, Esc twice: the menu (save first with F5).

## Building
| Tab / Shift + Tab | The next or previous tab of the building bar.
| Z, X, C, V, B | The slots of the bar from the left (then N, M and the keys , . /).
| Left click on a green hex | Build there. Hold Shift to place more of the same.
| Right click or Esc | Stop placing.
In the Roads & works tab pick a track or road (or a ditch, palisade, wall), then draw it hex by hex with the left button held.

## Windows and help
| 1 - 6 | Settlements, Research, Civilizations, Thinkers, Summary, Units (the same key closes it).
| P | The Units window, patrols first.
| T | The tutorial.
| G | Which routes show: none, orders under way, or those and the standing ones.
| L | A longer or shorter chronicle.
| H | The keys and the font.
| F1 | This manual.
| Page Up / Page Down | Scroll the open window or the side panel.

## The keyboard cursor
| Enter | Turn the cursor on: a yellow ring on a button of the screen (in the open window, if one is open).
| Arrows | Move the ring to the nearest button in that direction.
| Enter | Press the button under the ring. Its tooltip shows under the ring.
| Esc | Turn the cursor off.
With the cursor on, W A S D still move the map and Space still pauses. It reaches every button: the side panel's orders, the research cards, the peace window, the alerts' windows and the bar.

## Combat mode
| F | Combat mode on or off. The left button then picks soldiers only.
| Left click / drag | One soldier unit / every soldier in the box (Shift adds).
| Ctrl + left click | A line of one kind standing side by side.
| Shift + drag | Paint the hexes the selected soldiers guard.
| Right click | With several units: they go in formation (melee in front, archers behind, riders on the ends).
| Z / X / C | Stance: hold, aggressive, passive.
| V | Split the unit in half.
| B | Take the best arms of the settlement they stand in.
| N | Stop guarding.

## In the manual
| Up / Down | Scroll.
| Left / Right | The previous or next chapter.
| Esc or F1 | Close.

# First minutes
Turn on "Tutorial" in the start menu (or press `T`): a task list on screen leads through the first steps. In short:
- **Food first.** Build two Gatherers' huts (Food tab, key `Z`) on green hexes near the camp. One hut feeds few people.
- **Wood.** Build a Woodcutter: wood goes into nearly everything.
- **Research.** Open Research (`2`) and pick Dwellings. Research costs goods and time, one at a time.
- **Homes.** People grow only with free homes and food. Build Huts as soon as homes run out.
- **Look around.** Research Scouting, train scouts in the settlement's panel and send them out with a right click. What we never saw stays dark.
- **Prepare a defence.** For the first 15 minutes nobody attacks; after that other civilizations may come. Before then research Flint weapons and train a few warriors, or research Palisade and draw one around the camp.

People without work always gather around the settlement by themselves. Every building has places for workers; people fill free places on their own. A building can be paused or put first in its panel.

## Food, rations and winter
Once a minute each settlement feeds everyone who lives there and every unit that left from it. When food runs short the jobless go hungry first, then the workers, then the units; hungry people work slower, and after two missed rations some die. A settlement stops growing while its food falls and less than two rations are left.

Winter comes every 8 minutes and lasts 2: everybody works at half speed and gathering and fields give even less. People without clothing work slower still. Make clothing (Sewing, the Tailor) before it comes; an alert warns 30 seconds before and when there is too little clothing.

# Settlements and people
- **Levels:** Camp, Village, Town, City. Each level gives more land around the centre, homes and storage. Upgrade a settlement in its panel when it has the people, the technology and the goods (Village: Agriculture and 40 people; Town: Code of laws, Masonry and 150 people; City: Civil service, Construction and 400 people).
- **Goods missing for an upgrade** come by caravan from our other settlements by themselves.
- **New settlements:** research Migration, train Settlers and send them at least 5 hexes from any other settlement, on land nobody owns. They found a camp with the goods they carry.
- **Moving goods:** the settlement's panel offers "Bring 20 of a good" (one trip) and "Supply route" (a standing route) from the nearest settlement that has enough. Carriers walk the map, so roads make them faster.
- **Roads:** a track and later a road (Roads & works tab) make walking faster; with Masonry they bridge rivers.
- **Ruins** left by a civilization that is gone can be taken over.
- **Joining a settlement:** a unit standing in one of our settlements can join it ("Go home" in its panel). Soldiers can too ("Lay down arms"), but they lose their weapons, armour and supplies.
- **Better buildings:** select a building and press "Replace with another building" in its panel, then pick the new one in the bar.

# Research and eras
The eras are: Stone Age, Neolithic, Bronze Age, Iron Age, Middle Ages.

A technology costs goods, paid when its research starts, and takes some time. Its card in the research tree (hover it, or move the keyboard cursor onto it) says what it costs and, when it cannot be started yet, why. Research one technology at a time.

It is cheaper for every civilization we know that already knows it, and with a good thinker. Schools and academies speed up a branch.

## Getting to the next era
Our civilization enters the next era when it knows **5 technologies of the present era** (the basics of the Stone Age do not count) and its gateway technology:
- **To the Neolithic:** 5 Stone Age technologies.
- **To the Bronze Age:** 5 Neolithic technologies, Copper working among them.
- **To the Iron Age:** 5 Bronze Age technologies, Bronze working among them.
- **To the Middle Ages:** 5 Iron Age technologies, Iron working among them.
Some eras may also ask for goods made, like bronze or bricks; the tree's era heading says what is still missing. The top of the screen shows how many technologies are still missing ("0/5 to the Bronze Age"). Technologies of an era we have not reached cannot be started yet, but the whole tree is always visible.

## What each era brings
- **Stone Age:** gatherers, hunters, woodcutters, quarries, tools, huts, scouts, settlers and warriors.
- **Neolithic:** farms and pastures, pottery, masonry, weaving, the first trade, palisades, copper.
- **Bronze Age:** bronze, the wheel and caravans, writing and schools, horses, sailing, laws, archers and spearmen, barracks and stone walls.
- **Iron Age:** iron tools and weapons, swordsmen, construction, civil service, roads, engineering and catapults, bricks.
- **Middle Ages:** castles, knights, crossbows, mills, guilds and universities.

## Thinkers and advisors
Window `4`. We have one thinker at a time: a better one makes research cheaper. A thinker retires after a while and a successor takes over, a little weaker each generation. A new candidate shows up every few minutes.

An advisor (one at a time, hired for goods, eats a little food) gives one piece of advice at a time in its fields: economy, growth, research or expansion. Without an advisor nobody tells us what goes wrong.

# War and peace
## The army
Soldiers are trained in a settlement's panel. A soldier does not work and eats at every ration, and stays a soldier to the end. How many units we may have grows with the levels of our settlements, barracks, castles and some technologies. Every kind of unit is stronger on its own terrain. Arms and armour are goods: a unit takes the best ones of its settlement (key `B` in combat mode).

Experience belongs to a kind of unit (all archers, for example); at every level of it you choose one of three bonuses.

## Fighting
Select units and right click an enemy to attack. Combat mode (`F`) is for battles: pick soldiers with a box, send them in formation, set their stance, paint hexes to guard. Far from our land an army eats from its packs and the land; caravans can bring it food.

## Sieges
Surround an enemy settlement to cut it off: it works slower. Its units, the guard of its walls and some of its people defend it. When it falls, choose: occupy, loot or raze.

## Peace
A war ends with peace or when one side is gone. In the Civilizations window (`3`) propose peace: goods, maybe a settlement, and how long the peace lasts (3, 6 or 10 minutes). Only the side the war favours may demand; the other may offer a white peace or give something. "Suggest terms" fills in what the course of the war justifies. A long war tires a civilization: from half of the weariness bar the workshops work slower.

At most 3 wars at a time. An attack without declaring war, or breaking a peace, costs reputation. A civilization with a bad name finds others on guard against it: they keep more soldiers and seek alliances against it. Reputation slowly comes back in peace.

# Other civilizations
We meet other civilizations by seeing them: scouts, borders, their units. The Civilizations window (`3`) lists those we know: how they feel about us, our treaties, and the buttons for war, peace, a trade agreement, an alliance and exchange of goods (barter). A civilization's character shows only in what it does.

Scouts can go hidden: slower, but nobody can tell whose they are. When units of a civilization we have not met come near one of our settlements and have seen it, the alert **Strangers** tells us, but not who they are or where they come from.

# Tips
- Watch the red numbers on the goods bar: something is used faster than it is made.
- `J` goes through the alerts one by one.
- A building that stands still lacks workers or goods. Its panel says what is missing.
- Full stores waste work: build a Storehouse, or move the goods where they are needed.
- More people need more homes and more food at the same time.
- The Summary (`5`) after a game shows where our growth stopped.
